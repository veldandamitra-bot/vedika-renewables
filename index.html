Our approach

Measure first.
Design second.
Install once.

Every engagement starts with data: twelve months of bills, a load profile and a physical site survey. From there we model generation, quantify savings and only then recommend a system size. Efficiency measures are considered before capacity is added, because reducing demand is almost always cheaper than generating more.

After commissioning, monitoring keeps the asset honest — deviations from the modelled curve trigger a service visit rather than a surprise at the end of the year.

FAQ

Questions worth asking
before you sign anything.

Short, honest answers on installation, savings, maintenance, storage, timelines and what a consultation actually involves.

How long does an installation take?

A typical residential rooftop system is installed in a few days once approvals are in place. Commercial and industrial systems follow a project schedule shared with you at proposal stage — timelines depend on system size, structural conditions and grid connection requirements.

How much can I actually save?

Savings depend on your tariff, consumption pattern and roof area. We model expected generation and savings against your last twelve months of bills before you commit — so you see a realistic number, not a marketing figure.

What maintenance does a solar system need?

Periodic module cleaning, connection checks and inverter health reviews. We offer scheduled preventive maintenance plans and remote performance monitoring so issues are caught early, not at the end of the quarter.

Do I need battery storage (BESS)?

Not always. Storage makes sense when you need backup for critical loads, want to shift solar into peak hours, or are trying to displace diesel generation. We assess this as part of the feasibility study so the recommendation is based on your specific economics.

What are typical project timelines?

From consultation to commissioning, most projects follow a defined schedule covering survey, design, approvals, supply, installation and grid synchronisation. We share the schedule at proposal stage so you can plan accordingly.

What happens in a consultation?

We review your electricity bills, load profile, site constraints and goals, then outline realistic options, indicative costs and a timeline. There is no obligation to proceed — the goal is to give you an honest view of what is feasible and what it would actually cost.
